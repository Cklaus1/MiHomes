# Hiring Playbook — Household Staff

**Type**: hiring  
**Owner**: EA (Millena)  
**Properties**: all  
**Triggers**: open role, staff departure, expansion

This playbook covers the full hiring cycle for household staff — from posting through first day. Do not skip steps. Background checks and confidentiality agreements are non-negotiable before anyone enters the property.

---

## Phase 1: Job Posting

### Write the Job Description
- [ ] Define the role clearly: title, primary property, hours/schedule, compensation range
- [ ] List core responsibilities (reference the relevant playbook — housekeeper, daily ops, etc.)
- [ ] List requirements: experience, transportation, background check consent, language
- [ ] State what's NOT included (what vendors handle vs staff)
- [ ] Include: "Must pass background check. Confidentiality agreement required."

**Job description lives at**: `knowledge/staff/job-descriptions/<role>.md`

### Where to Post
- [ ] Indeed (primary — broadest reach)
- [ ] Local household staffing agencies (faster, pre-vetted, higher cost)
- [ ] Referrals — ask current/former trusted staff first (best source)
- [ ] Nextdoor or local community boards (for part-time roles)
- [ ] Estate staffing agencies for senior roles (property manager, house manager)

### Screening Filter (before phone call)
Reject applications that:
- Have no prior in-home or hospitality experience
- Cannot provide 2+ verifiable references from household employers
- Do not have reliable transportation (if required)
- Have unexplained multi-month employment gaps without context

Move forward with: anyone who clears the above and has a coherent work history.

---

## Phase 2: Phone Screening (20–30 min)

**Goal**: Assess communication, reliability, and fit — not just experience. Listen for how they talk about previous employers. Red flags are often in tone, not content.

**Conducted by**: Millena  
**Notes go in**: `knowledge/staff/candidates/<name>-<date>.md`

### Opening (2 min)
- Introduce yourself, explain the role briefly
- Confirm they're still interested and available on the proposed schedule

### Experience
- [ ] "Walk me through your last two positions in residential cleaning."
- [ ] "What were your main responsibilities day-to-day?"

### Standards & Work Style
- [ ] "How do you know when a room is actually clean — what's your standard?"
- [ ] "When you find something broken or damaged, what do you do?"
- [ ] "What does a good day look like for you? A hard day?"
- [ ] "Have you ever had to handle an emergency — a leak, power outage, anything unexpected? What happened?"

### Reliability & Logistics
- [ ] "What's your transportation situation — do you drive?"
- [ ] "Have you ever had to call out unexpectedly? How do you handle that?"
- [ ] "Are you comfortable with the schedule: [days/hours]?"
- [ ] "What's your availability for a start date if offered the role?"

### Privacy & Fit
- [ ] "This is a private residence. We require full confidentiality — no photos, no discussion of the property or family outside of work. Are you comfortable with that?"
- [ ] "We use a task management system. Are you comfortable learning and using an app?"
- [ ] "What's your compensation expectation?"
- [ ] "Why are you leaving your current position?" *(listen carefully)*

### Close
- [ ] Explain next steps: in-person trial day, background check, references
- [ ] Ask: "Do you have any questions for me?"
- [ ] Set timeline: "We'll follow up within [X] days."

### Scoring (internal — do not share)
Rate 1–5 on each after the call:

| Dimension | Score |
|-----------|-------|
| Relevant experience | /5 |
| Clarity of standards | /5 |
| Communication style | /5 |
| Reliability signals | /5 |
| Privacy / discretion comfort | /5 |
| **Total** | /25 |

**Move forward** with scores of 18+. Flag any 1s as automatic holds regardless of total.

---

## Red Flags — Stop or Proceed with Caution

**Stop immediately:**
- Reluctant to consent to background check
- Cannot provide any references from household employment
- Speaks negatively or dismissively about previous employers
- Vague or inconsistent about why they left prior roles
- "I'm very good with kids / pets" without being asked — oversharing personal interest

**Proceed with caution:**
- Only one verifiable reference (ask why)
- Short tenure at multiple positions (ask about each)
- Heavy focus on duties they prefer vs. all duties required
- Needs significant schedule flexibility that conflicts with role

## Phase 3: Narrow to Top 2–3

- [ ] Review scores and notes from all phone screens
- [ ] Eliminate anyone with red flags
- [ ] Select top 2–3 based on: score, availability, reliability signals, gut read
- [ ] Prepare a short candidate summary for owner review:

```
Candidate Summary — [Role]
Date: [date]

1. [Name] — Score: 22/25
   Experience: 6 years, two private homes (3 and 4 years each)
   Availability: Immediate, Mon–Fri 8am–4pm
   Strengths: Clear standards, handled leak emergency well, excellent discretion
   Concern: None significant
   References: 2 confirmed household employers

2. [Name] — Score: 19/25
   ...
```

- [ ] Send candidate summary to Chris for review
- [ ] Get go-ahead on which candidates to advance

## Phase 4: Trial Day (Working Interview)

**Critical step. Do not skip.**  
A trial day shows more than any interview. You're watching standards, pace, initiative, and how they handle ambiguity — not just whether they're pleasant to talk to.

- [ ] Schedule a paid half-day (4 hours) — confirm in writing
- [ ] Brief them on what to expect: "You'll do a standard cleaning round. We'll observe occasionally but let you work."
- [ ] Assign the same tasks you'd assign on day one
- [ ] Do not over-guide — see what they do independently

### What to Observe
- Do they follow a logical sequence or jump randomly?
- Do they check in when uncertain, or just guess?
- Do they move at a working pace or need prompting?
- Do they notice things that weren't on the list (proactive)?
- How do they handle a room they've never been in before?
- Do they ask appropriate questions (not too many, not zero)?

### Evaluation (complete same day)
- [ ] Fill out trial day evaluation (use same 5-dimension scoring above)
- [ ] Note anything that would disqualify or elevate the candidate
- [ ] Make your recommendation before discussing with anyone (anchoring bias)

---

## Phase 5: Reference Checks

**Call — do not email. People say things they won't write.**

- [ ] Call 2+ references, minimum 1 from a household employer
- [ ] Confirm: dates of employment, role, property type
- [ ] Ask:
  - "Would you rehire this person?" *(pause after, let them fill silence)*
  - "What were they best at?"
  - "What would you coach them to improve?"
  - "Were there ever any issues with reliability or attendance?"
  - "Was there anything that surprised you — positively or negatively — during their time with you?"
  - "Is there anything I should know that I haven't asked?"

**If a reference won't confirm employment dates or seems guarded**: flag immediately, do not move forward without explanation.

---

## Phase 6: Background Check

- [ ] Get signed background check authorization before running (required by law)
- [ ] Run check through the payroll company (annual background checks are handled there)
- [ ] Check includes: criminal history, identity verification, SSN validation
- [ ] For driving roles: motor vehicle record (MVR)
- [ ] Review results — anything requiring a decision goes to Chris
- [ ] Do not make an offer until check is returned clean
- [ ] Note: all existing staff are subject to annual re-checks through the payroll company — calendar this on hire date each year

---

## Phase 7: Owner Meeting & Decision

- [ ] Schedule 20-min call with Chris — present top candidate(s)
- [ ] Share: candidate summary, trial day notes, reference check summary, background check status
- [ ] Chris makes final call
- [ ] If two candidates are close: Chris may want a brief intro call with each

---

## Phase 8: Offer & Paperwork

- [ ] Verbal offer first — confirm acceptance before sending paperwork
- [ ] Written offer letter includes: title, start date, hourly/salary, hours, property assignment, benefits (if any), at-will statement
- [ ] Send for signature via [DocuSign / email / print]
- [ ] Collect before Day 1:
  - [ ] Signed offer letter
  - [ ] I-9 (with ID documents on Day 1)
  - [ ] W-4 (federal) and state equivalent
  - [ ] Direct deposit authorization
  - [ ] Emergency contact form
  - [ ] **Confidentiality / NDA agreement** — signed before entering property
- [ ] Add to payroll system

---

## Phase 9: Handoff to Onboarding

- [ ] Confirm start date with new hire
- [ ] Trigger the onboarding playbook: `mihomes playbook run onboarding-new-hire --property <slug> --start <date>`
- [ ] Brief existing staff: name, start date, role — nothing else
- [ ] Assign onboarding buddy (senior staff member)
- [ ] Prepare access: entry code, alarm code, staff WhatsApp group

---

## Hiring Timeline (target)

| Step | Timeline |
|------|----------|
| Post job | Day 0 |
| Initial application review | Day 3–5 |
| Phone screens (all) | Day 5–10 |
| Candidate summary to Chris | Day 11 |
| Trial days | Day 12–15 |
| Reference + background checks | Day 13–16 |
| Owner meeting | Day 16–17 |
| Offer sent | Day 17–18 |
| Start date | Day 28–35 |

Total cycle: ~4–5 weeks. Compress if urgent, but don't skip steps — a bad hire in a private home is much harder to undo than a slow hire.
